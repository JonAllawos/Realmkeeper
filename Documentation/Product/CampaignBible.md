# Realmkeeper Campaign Bible — Working Edition

**Updated:** November 13, 2026
**Campaign foundation:** Rounds 1–8
**Implementation reference:** Phase 22 completion report (`../Reports/Phase22-Completion-Report.md`)
**Status:** Living document — undecided choices remain open

## 1. Purpose and canon rules

This document is the central reference for Realmkeeper's campaign, world, characters, mythology, progression, and narrative direction.

It distinguishes between:

- **Established canon:** Decisions explicitly made by the creator.
- **Proposed development:** Suggestions that may be adopted later.
- **Undecided:** Questions that remain open.
- **Implementation status:** Features confirmed by development reports, not necessarily narrative canon.

No proposed idea becomes canon without approval. No undecided question should be silently resolved by later writing or implementation.

The campaign and sandbox must exist within the same persistent world. The world should continue functioning whether or not the player follows the main story.

## 2. Core campaign identity

### Established direction

Realmkeeper is a dark fantasy RPG combining:

- Grounded medieval life and high fantasy.
- A dangerous, morally complicated world.
- An ancient supernatural mystery.
- A world-spanning threat.
- Political conflicts and competing factions.
- Deep companion relationships and personal stories.
- A central campaign alongside a substantial open-world sandbox.
- Persistent consequences at personal, settlement, faction, regional, and world levels.

The player can spend considerable time away from the main storyline. Exploration, crafting, building relationships, earning experience, and participating in faction or settlement activities are legitimate parts of the game.

The campaign should not force the player into a single predetermined heroic identity.

## 3. The protagonist

### Established canon

The protagonist has a criminal background, particularly involving smuggling goods across borders.

They were hired to provide information or assistance at a military outpost, fortress, or border garrison.

Their background provides useful contacts, skills, and reputation in certain regions, but they are not universally famous.

The protagonist's criminal history does not predetermine their morality, personality, class, or future allegiances.

### Opening events

The protagonist arrives as part of an established criminal crew.

Their operation involves unusual or prohibited artifacts originating from forgotten ruins.

During an infiltration sequence, they discover evidence of a secret military operation involving a much older and more powerful unknown object.

The military attempts to operate or activate the object.

A supernatural catastrophe follows. Ancient creatures emerge, part of the garrison is destroyed or possessed, and the protagonist's crew is scattered.

The protagonist survives but does not understand the true cause or significance of what happened.

The crew members' ultimate fates remain open. They may escape, be captured, die, betray the protagonist, or disappear.

The opening should transition from stealth and infiltration into a dangerous battle and survival sequence.

### Narrative principle

The protagonist knows what they personally witnessed but does not understand what it means.

The mystery must be reconstructed through evidence, exploration, archaeological discoveries, testimony, conflicting records, and the actions of surviving characters.

## 4. The ancient mythology

### Established canon

An ancient civilization existed before recorded history.

This civilization entered another ancient world while searching for resources or a new source of power.

It brought back an artifact or technology capable of altering reality.

The civilization experimented with controlling or domesticating organisms from that other world.

These experiments permanently altered the boundary between the two worlds.

The ancient civilization eventually collapsed following its experiments with the containment system.

The containment system exists to separate the two worlds because they would otherwise merge.

### Historical sequence

1. An ancient civilization exists before recorded history.
2. It discovers or gains access to a connection to another world.
3. It enters that world to seek resources and power.
4. It brings back reality-altering technology or an artifact.
5. It experiments with controlling or domesticating organisms from the other world.
6. These experiments permanently alter the boundary between the worlds.
7. A containment system is established or used to keep the worlds separate.
8. Experiments involving that system contribute to the civilization's collapse.
9. The civilization disappears from recorded history.
10. Much later, a modern military research group rediscovers ancient technology.
11. The group attempts to harness ancient energy or magic without understanding the danger.
12. Its activation awakens the ancient system and allows predatory creatures to return.

This sequence is the established broad history. The exact dates, order of individual experiments, and precise cause of the collapse remain undecided.

### Important distinctions

Three concepts must remain distinct unless later decisions explicitly connect them:

- **Reality-altering artifact:** Technology brought back from the ancient world.
- **Ancient experiments:** Attempts to control or domesticate organisms from that world.
- **Containment system:** Infrastructure intended to prevent the two worlds from merging.

They may be interconnected, but they are not automatically the same object.

## 5. The ancient world

### Established canon

The other world:

- Existed before our civilization.
- Contains radically different regions and environments.
- Has multiple intelligent species.
- Contains societies with alliances and rivalries.
- Is the place of origin of the returning creatures.
- Can eventually be entered through several ancient gateways in different regions.

It is not merely a monster dimension or a collection of disconnected dungeons. It is an independently existing world with its own history.

### Design principles

Its regions should eventually have distinct:

- Geography and climate.
- Ecosystems and wildlife.
- Intelligent inhabitants.
- Political powers and relationships.
- Settlements and architecture.
- Ruins and historical mysteries.
- Resources and economic systems.
- Exploration opportunities and dangers.

The ancient world must have existed independently of our civilization. Our civilization entered an existing world; it did not create all of its inhabitants, history, or conflicts.

### Undecided

- The ancient world's name.
- Its overall condition today.
- The number and identities of its intelligent species.
- Their physical appearances and cultures.
- Their political systems and relationships.
- Whether any ancient civilization remains from before the first crossing.
- Whether the world contains other intelligent or supernatural entities beyond the known species.

## 6. The containment system

### Established canon

The containment system is intended to separate two worlds that would otherwise merge.

The ancient civilization's experiments permanently altered their boundary.

The modern military's activation of an ancient object awakens the system and contributes to the return of creatures from the other world.

The player eventually gains the ability to control the containment system and decide how it should be used.

Doing so requires finding and restoring several ancient structures throughout the world.

The player eventually accesses the other world through several gateways in different regions.

### Player progression toward control

The eventual objective should involve:

1. Discovering ancient structures.
2. Exploring and accessing them.
3. Recovering the knowledge needed to understand their purpose.
4. Finding the resources and components needed for restoration.
5. Restoring individual structures.
6. Changing the state of the wider containment system.
7. Gaining the ability to make decisions about the relationship between the worlds.

Restoration must have meaningful consequences. It should not simply fill a progress bar.

### Proposed possibilities, not canon

Potential outcomes include strict separation, controlled passage, gradual integration, shared governance, or an alternative solution.

No ending philosophy has been selected.

## 7. Returning creatures and the outbreak

### Established canon

The returning creatures are monstrous predators originating from the ancient world.

Their return is connected to the awakening of the ancient object.

The initial outbreak is limited to the region around the military fortress. It spreads gradually.

Ordinary people initially understand the events as a new monster outbreak with no known explanation.

### Proposed creature-design principles

Creature ecology should be coherent rather than a collection of unrelated monsters.

Potential ecological roles include:

- Small hunters and scavengers.
- Pack predators.
- Burrowing predators.
- Territorial creatures.
- Large apex predators.
- Unusual organisms adapted to specific regions.

Different creatures should have distinct behaviors, habitats, sensory capabilities, and hunting strategies.

The existence of predatory creatures does not establish that every species in the other world is hostile.

### World consequences

The outbreak should affect:

- Patrols and military operations.
- Caravans and trade routes.
- Livestock and agriculture.
- Wilderness exploration.
- Settlement defenses.
- Refugee movements.
- Regional supplies and prices.
- Faction relationships and political decisions.

These consequences should emerge from the persistent world simulation rather than being limited to scripted quest dialogue.

## 8. The military conspiracy

### Established canon

A secret group within the military knowingly took an enormous risk.

It wanted to harness ancient energy or magic.

It understood almost nothing about the actual danger.

It was involved in operating or activating the unknown object at the fortress.

### Proposed organizational direction

The secret project could exist within a broader military institution rather than being a completely separate organization.

Possible participants include officers, researchers, field operatives, logistical personnel, and political sponsors.

Different participants may have conflicting motivations and varying levels of responsibility.

The organization should not automatically be treated as uniformly evil.

Some members may conceal the disaster, some may attempt to correct it, and others may continue pursuing the technology.

### Undecided

- The group's name.
- Its leadership.
- Its relationship to the broader military command.
- Its exact intended application for the energy.
- Whether it had political sponsors.
- Whether surviving members remain active.
- Whether other factions know about the project.

## 9. Factions, politics, and ordinary life

### Established direction

Faction decisions determine allies, enemies, and eventually aspects of the political landscape.

The world contains multiple cultures and powers.

Ordinary people initially interpret the crisis as an unexplained monster outbreak.

Different groups may develop different explanations as evidence accumulates.

### Design principles

Factions should have their own objectives, resources, relationships, leadership conflicts, and responses to world events.

The player should be able to work with factions, oppose them, manipulate them, remain independent, or change their political position.

The criminal network should provide an alternative information and interaction system.

The protagonist's contacts may learn about disrupted routes, missing caravans, unusual artifact sales, and military activity before official authorities acknowledge the full situation.

## 10. Companions and the scattered crew

### Established direction

Companions should have substantial personal identities and relationships.

Their stories should include:

- Personal quests.
- Conflicting interests.
- Relationships with the player.
- Party dynamics.
- Meaningful reactions to player decisions.
- Romance where appropriate.

The original criminal crew is scattered during the opening catastrophe.

Recovering its members is a potential early campaign thread.

The identities, roles, individual histories, and fates of the crew remain undecided.

Companions must not be interchangeable combat units. They should have personal goals and independent reactions to the world.

## 11. Campaign structure

### Established direction

The campaign combines a central storyline with an expansive sandbox.

Side quests can include short activities and long multi-part chains.

Consequences may affect individuals, settlements, factions, regions, and the world.

The main campaign reaches a significant conclusion, but the world remains playable afterward.

Some optional stories may remain unresolved.

### Proposed broad structure

1. **The Awakening:** Infiltration, military experiment, catastrophe, survival.
2. **The Scattered:** Recovering contacts and crew members, investigating the regional outbreak.
3. **The Unquiet Frontier:** Regional threats, settlements, trade, and military response.
4. **Relics of the Forgotten:** Ancient ruins, artifacts, and archaeological discoveries.
5. **The Hidden Operation:** Understanding the military conspiracy.
6. **Beyond the Boundary:** Restoring structures and entering the ancient world.
7. **The Ancient Truth:** Reconstructing the ancient civilization's experiments and collapse.
8. **The World's Fate:** Gaining control of the containment system and deciding its future.
9. **The Aftermath:** A persistent world reflecting the player's decisions.

These are structural placeholders, not finalized chapter names or quest lists.

### Implementation note (not canon)

- **One campaign system already exists.** Phase 17 built an authored-campaign system over the persistent world: chapters, arcs, decisions with delayed consequences, endings, and companions in the story (`../Systems/Campaigns.md`, ADR-0082 to ADR-0087). This campaign is intended to be authored on that system, not on a new one.
- **"Waystone" is test content.** The demonstration campaign shipped with Phase 17 is not canon.
- **The opening and the new-game flow.** Today a new game places the player in a town as "pending", able to walk, trade and talk before creating a character, and no experience is earned until creation. The opening described in §3 implies that creation comes first and the story begins at once. The new-game flow will need to change when the opening is built. That is a design decision still to make; nothing has been changed for it.

## 12. Character progression and Phase 22 implementation

### Confirmed implementation from Phase 22

Phase 22 establishes the current character-creation and level-up foundation.

#### Starting character rules

- New characters begin at level 1.
- New characters begin with zero experience.
- Experience awards before character creation are refused.
- Existing saves retain their historical experience.
- Every new character is checked against the level 1 class baseline before creation commits.
- Higher-level choices and subclasses cannot be introduced through character-creation shortcuts or templates.
- Species traits are gated by their minimum required level.
- Later-level features are represented as locked rather than granted prematurely.

#### Player-controlled advancement

- Experience makes a character eligible for advancement, but does not automatically level them.
- The player receives a level-ready notification.
- A HUD button opens the level-up process.
- The level-up interface separates automatic benefits from player choices.
- The player makes the required selections. Choices are preserved when the window closes or the game saves, and advancement resumes when reopened.
- Confirming applies the level once.
- The player is not automatically assigned choices.

### Campaign implications

The campaign must respect the implemented player-driven advancement model.

Quest rewards, combat rewards, exploration rewards, and other XP sources should contribute to eligibility without bypassing the level-up process.

The main campaign should provide enough legitimate progression opportunities for a player who focuses primarily on the main story.

Side quests, exploration, crafting, and other activities should offer additional meaningful ways to earn experience.

The campaign should not require repetitive filler activities to remain appropriately leveled.

Level progression should not be confused with narrative knowledge or faction reputation. A character may understand an ancient mechanism, gain political influence, or earn access to a location without automatically gaining a character level.

### Progression design still to establish

- The exact XP curve and level milestones.
- XP rewards for major and minor quest categories.
- How crafting, exploration, discoveries, and non-combat activities award XP.
- Recommended levels for campaign chapters and major encounters.
- Which main-story progression gates require character capability, knowledge, resources, or allies.
- How limited timed quests interact with player freedom.
- Whether specific campaign events award unique abilities or non-level-based progression.

The campaign must not assume that side quests are mandatory for leveling.

## 13. Character creation rules and current implementation limitations

Phase 22 also confirms:

- The character creator reviews level 1 statistics and starting features.
- Tiefling spellcasting ability can be selected.
- Later tiefling legacy spells are represented and locked until their required levels.
- Some ancestry abilities are implemented, while others remain deferred.
- The goliath's Stone's Endurance and Storm's Thunder now answer spell damage as well as attacks (playable).
- The level-up workflow is persistent across saving and loading.
- Save schema v24 includes pending advancement and level-up notification state.

### Known deferred features

Ancestry:

- Gnome forest and rock ancestry spells.
- Tiefling Thaumaturgy mechanics.
- Tiefling level 3 and 5 innate spellcasting mechanics.
- Dragonborn breath weapon as a proper non-spell combat action.
- Ancestry reactions against hazards outside combat.

Class features and other systems:

- Hellish Rebuke (a warlock spell, not an ancestry trait) still triggers only on attack damage, not spell damage.
- Only SRD subclasses are currently offered; broader subclass support is deferred.
- The shop economy is recorded as a future requirement rather than implemented.

Campaign writing and quests must not assume these deferred abilities are playable.

## 15. Design principles that must remain consistent

1. The world exists independently of the player.
2. The campaign and sandbox share one persistent world.
3. The player is not automatically a chosen one.
4. The protagonist's criminal background provides opportunities without dictating morality.
5. Ancient history is uncovered gradually.
6. Different cultures may preserve contradictory interpretations of the past.
7. The ancient world has its own history and political conflicts.
8. Not every creature or intelligent species is hostile.
9. Factions have independent motivations.
10. The main campaign must not require repetitive side content to progress.
11. Player-driven advancement must remain meaningful.
12. World events should create persistent simulation consequences.
13. Major decisions should have consequences at multiple scales.
14. The final control of the containment system must be earned through exploration, restoration, and understanding.
15. The ending must reflect player decisions rather than a predetermined moral answer.
16. Undecided lore must remain undecided until explicitly approved.
